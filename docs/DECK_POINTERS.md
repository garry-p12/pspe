# PSPE — slide pointers

A deck outline for a mixed audience: people who will not read a loss function,
sitting beside people who will ask what the confidence interval was. Every
slide below gives **the plain sentence** (say this out loud), **the technical
backing** (have this ready for the question), and **the figure**.

Figures are in `docs/figures/deck/` as `.svg`, `.pdf` and `.png`, regenerated by
`python scripts/make_deck_figures.py`. Figures named `pspe_*` are the existing
thesis set in `docs/figures/`.

Numbers carry their section (`§`) so any claim can be traced back to
`docs/THESIS_AND_RESULTS.md`. **Nothing on these slides is rounded in our
favour, and the places where we lose are on their own slides, not in a
footnote.**

---

## Act I — The problem

### 1. Title
- **PSPE: decision systems that are measured against reality, not against their own model.**
- One line of context: we build systems that forecast a hazard — wildfire,
  flood — and then *act* on it: where to cut a firebreak, when to run a pump,
  when to tell a neighbourhood to leave.
- *No figure. Say the thesis sentence and stop.*

### 2. The thing that goes wrong
- **Plain:** every part of the system reports that it is working, and the
  decision still gets worse.
- **Technical:** each component's metric is computed *inside the model's
  account of the world*. Forecast skill, reconstruction accuracy, surrogate
  cost — all are self-assessments.
- The uncomfortable version: a model that matches the hidden state *better* can
  plan *worse*. We measured that, three times, in opposite directions from
  what the component metric predicted.
- **Figure:** `deck_measurement`

### 3. Seven silent failures
- Walk two rows only. Suggested pair, because they are the easiest to feel:
  - *"the surrogate said the budget was satisfied — 7.3% of real runs broke it"* (§3.1)
  - *"the forecaster had high one-step skill — the rollout invented a fire that never happened"* (§5.2)
- **Plain:** the system was never lying. It was answering a different question
  from the one we needed.
- **Technical:** the map from component metric to decision quality is not
  monotone, and in three measured cases it is **inverted**.
- Honest framing: several of these have precedent in the literature. The
  contribution is the catalogue and the corrections, not discovering the genre.
- **Figure:** `deck_measurement`, or the thesis `pspe_silent_failure`

## Act II — The method

### 4. The idea in one sentence
- **Plain:** we have a cheap way to get a direction that is slightly wrong, and
  an expensive way to get one that is right but noisy. Use both, and let the
  data decide the blend.
- **Technical:** a pathwise gradient through a differentiable surrogate
  (low variance, biased by model error) mixed with a likelihood-ratio gradient
  from the real environment (unbiased, high variance).
- **Figure:** `deck_framework`

### 5. The mixing rule
- The weight is not a hyperparameter. It is chosen to minimise the variance of
  the combined estimator:

  **α\* = (V_lr − Cov) / (B² + V_pw + V_lr − 2·Cov)**

- **Plain:** when the model is trustworthy, lean on it. When it is not, pay for
  real samples. The formula works that out continuously instead of us guessing.
- **Technical:** `B²` is the measured pathwise bias, `‖g_sur − g_truth‖²`, not
  an assumed constant — it is *measured* against truth rollouts.
- **Say the catch out loud:** measuring `B²` costs real rollouts, so the arm
  that uses it is not on the same budget as the arms that do not. That matters
  on slide 11.
- **Figure:** `deck_framework`

### 6. Safety: the dual
- **Plain:** a thermostat on the constraint. Over budget, push harder; back
  under, ease off.
- **Technical:** PID control on the Lagrange multiplier, with gains scaled to
  the *reward* gradient.
- **Where it fails, and we show this later:** if the policy runs to the edge of
  its action range, every gradient through it vanishes and λ multiplies
  nothing. See slide 20.

### 7. The part that is actually the contribution
- **Plain:** we check the system against the world, on a schedule, and we let
  that check overrule the model.
- **Technical:** three measurement paths — probe the true environment
  periodically; synchronise the model state to observation; score the belief on
  the *decision*, not on reconstruction.
- **Figure:** `pspe_system` (existing thesis figure)

## Act III — Does it work

### 8. The headline
- **Plain:** given the same amount of real-world data, our method beats all four
  of the standard safe-RL algorithms. On both test families.
- **Technical:** §3.4n, Vista job 1049073. 20 seeds, seed-paired, 20,000-resample
  bootstrap, every arm compared against a do-nothing policy on its own initial
  conditions.
- rdf: **+0.50** vs do-nothing; next best (Sauté) +0.11. Paired against each
  baseline, all four intervals exclude zero.
- dar: **+0.0866** on 20/20 seeds; the best baseline manages +0.0066, and
  PPO-Lagrangian gets **+0.0001** — literally at the null.
- **Figure:** `deck_matched_budget`

### 9. Why "matched budget" is the whole point
- **Plain:** the usual way to report this is "we used 12× less data". That is an
  *input*, not a result. The real question is what each method does when you
  give them all the same amount.
- **Technical:** PPO-Lagrangian is statistically indistinguishable from us at
  its full 38,400 transitions (+0.070, CI [−0.039, +0.167]); at our ~8,300 it is
  +0.417 behind. **It needs 4.6× more data to draw level.**
- This reversed one of our own published verdicts. Say so.
- **Figure:** `deck_matched_budget`

### 10. A benchmark that could not rank anything
- **Plain:** on one test problem, three of the four published methods cannot be
  shown to beat *doing nothing at all*.
- **Technical:** §3.4l, dar at full budget. CPO +0.0116 [−0.0012, +0.0238],
  PPO-Lagrangian +0.0141 [−0.0050, +0.0322], primal-dual NPG +0.0096
  [−0.0039, +0.0226] — three intervals straddling zero.
- **Why this is on a slide:** it is a problem with the *benchmark*, and we
  found it in the benchmark we were using to make ourselves look good.
- **Figure:** `deck_null_floor`

### 11. The safety result
- **Plain:** we cut constraint violations roughly threefold, and we can say how
  often we expect to fail.
- **Technical:** §3.2b, 20 seeds. 14.1% → **5.0%** violating. Seed-paired effect
  −9.1 points [−11.8, −5.5], t = −4.59, p = 0.0002, better on 15 of 20 seeds.
- **The detail that matters:** a conformal margin *on its own* does nothing —
  0 of 20 seeds improve. It is the **probe** that makes it bite. Conformalise
  the distribution that actually violates, not the model's error.
- **Figure:** `deck_margin`

### 12. The mixing rule, honestly
- **Plain:** adaptive α does not make the system score better on average. It
  stops it blowing up.
- **Technical:** §4.2a, 20 seeds. Violations: fixed α **5/20 seeds**, variance
  rule **1/20**, Eq. 8 **0/20**. But the only *matched-budget* comparison —
  variance rule vs fixed α at 5,120 samples each — is **p = 0.083**, which does
  not separate on the mean.
- **Say this plainly:** α is a **variance effect, not a mean effect**. One
  fixed-α seed diverged to cost 8.1 with λ = 19.1; the adaptive rules did not.
  That is the claim, and it is smaller than the earlier draft of this work said.
- **Figure:** `deck_mixing_rule`

## Act IV — Does it work on real things

### 13. Wildfire, on observed records
- **Plain:** given real fire records, the planner places firebreaks better than
  the operational rule of thumb.
- **Technical:** §5.1. NDWS, U-Net surrogate frozen, 8×8 grid of firebreak
  intensities, 3%-of-patch daily crew budget, population-weighted burn
  objective. **Paired t = 11.15, mean gap +12.0 points.**
- **The honest caveat, on the same slide:** prior work on Cell2Fire can evaluate
  the counterfactual exactly because their simulator *defines* the
  intervention. Ours is fitted to what real fires did, so we cannot. It is a
  trade-off, not a win.

### 14. The twin loop, on satellite sequences
- **Plain:** feeding real observations back into the model keeps it honest.
  Without that, it drifts and invents fires.
- **Technical:** §5.2. State sync 0.608 ± 0.110 against open-loop 0.085 ± 0.047
  at one day — **8.4×**, on every fire.
- **Read it as a control, not a finding** (§9.8): it mostly demonstrates the
  loop is wired correctly.
- **And the correction:** at 8 fires the loop looked like t = 3.0 on 8 of 8. At
  **607 fires** the same benefit is **a third the size** and holds on 76%
  (§5.2a). Same experiment, 75× the sample, much smaller claim.

### 15. The flood digital twin — what it is
- **Plain:** a live portal for Onion Creek, Austin. Real terrain, real roads,
  real river gauges. Ask it what a storm does, where to build, who gets cut off.
- **Technical:** LISFLOOD-FP local-inertial shallow-water solver on USGS 3DEP
  bare-earth DEM, OpenStreetMap road network, live USGS gauge feed.
- **Figure:** `pspe_app_flood` (existing), or a portal screenshot

### 16. One detail worth the whole slide — the DEM
- **Plain:** the default elevation data includes trees and buildings. Water
  ponds on treetops.
- **Technical:** Copernicus GLO-30 is a *surface* model; the shipped design
  storm floods **28.84 km²** on it against **18.71 km²** on USGS 3DEP bare
  earth. A finer grid makes it *worse*, because more resolution means more
  spurious canopy.
- **Why it is here:** this is the catalogue thesis in miniature. Every number
  was self-consistent, and wrong, until it was checked against what the ground
  actually is.

### 17. Warning time — measured, not modelled
- **Plain:** a map of which roads flood does not tell you whether anyone would
  know in time. So we measured it from the gauge record.
- **Technical:** lag between the upstream gauge's peak and the outlet's, on real
  floods. Median **4 h 45 min**.
- **The number under the number:** that median is over the four floods the gauge
  *recorded*. The two largest events since it was built — 2013-10-31 at
  3,823 m³/s and 2015-10-30 at 3,455 m³/s — left **no usable trace at all**.
  And one of the four it did see gave **20 minutes**.
- **Say this:** a median over the days the instrument worked describes the
  warning available during the floods nobody needed warning for.
- **Figure:** `deck_warning`

## Act V — The RL gym, and a negative result we did not want

### 18. Turning the twin into a decision problem
- **Plain:** two real decisions, not toy ones. *Operate*: when to open a gate
  and run a pump on a polder. *Evacuate*: when to tell which neighbourhoods to
  leave.
- **Technical:** both expose the repo's batched protocol and a Gymnasium view
  (`info["cost"]`, Safety-Gymnasium convention), so outside algorithms run on
  them unmodified. `gymnasium.utils.env_checker` passes.
- The evacuation environment precomputes egress by real time-dependent routing
  over the 3,659-segment network, so a step is a lookup and an episode costs
  microseconds while the routing stays real.

### 19. The baseline nobody beat
- **Plain:** on the polder, a thermostat beats every learned method we tried.
- **Technical:** budget 2.0 pump-hours. Level deadband **−41.50 at 0.44**
  pump-hours, feasible. Three of four safe-RL baselines finish **over budget** —
  PPO-Lagrangian and primal-dual NPG violate on **every** evaluation episode —
  and still score below it.
- **Plain, again:** a constrained method that lands outside its constraint is
  not a weaker solution. It is not a solution.
- **Figure:** `deck_polder`

### 20. Why our own planner failed, and the guard that was switched off
- **Plain:** the planner pushed its controls to maximum and stayed there. The
  safety thermostat screamed and nothing happened.
- **Technical:** the policy's pre-tanh mean reached **1,436**. Past about 3,
  `tanh` is flat, so every gradient through the policy vanishes and λ — which
  reached 27.7 and was still climbing linearly — multiplies nothing. Cost pinned
  at the ceiling from iteration 50.
- **The sharp bit:** `PlannerConfig` already documents this exact failure, for a
  different problem family, right above the guard that prevents it — and leaves
  the guard off by default. Every flood result in this project ran with it off.
- Turning it on: pre-tanh settles at **1.9**, cost comes **inside budget at
  1.62**, and λ rises to 32 then *releases* to 20. The mechanism works.
- **And it still does not win:** feasible at **−60.62**, against the deadband's
  −41.50. One seed; the eight-seed version is running.
- **Figure:** `deck_collapse`

### 21. The heuristic is good because the problem is small
- **Plain:** the one-number trigger looks unbeatable partly because there is
  almost nothing to decide.
- **Technical:** swept 70 thresholds from 0.30 to 0.99. The family has **three
  distinct behaviours**. Between cost 6.51 and 15.19 it returns **exactly one
  value**, −3511.4, at any threshold. Thirty consecutive thresholds give the
  same number to one decimal while cost varies 2.3×.
- **So the fair question** is not "can RL beat the trigger" but "can it sit
  *between* the trigger's steps", which are 1,100 return apart.
- **Figure:** `deck_flood_frontier`

### 22. What the evacuation result actually is
- **Plain:** at the same cost, the learned policies come out slightly ahead —
  and not by enough to call it.
- **Technical:** 8 seeds for PPO-Lagrangian, scored against the best trigger
  *each seed could afford*: **+491 ± 513** (t = 0.96, wins 5/8, sign p = 0.73).
  CPO **+708 ± 647** (3/4, p = 0.63).
- **The correction worth making in public:** the earlier version of this
  comparison scored learned arms against a trigger spending **2.3× as much**.
  Fixing that flips the *sign* of the point estimate — and changes no
  conclusion, because neither direction was ever significant.

## Act VI — What we do not claim

### 23. The trade we make
- **Plain:** we get more done, and we cross the line more often than the
  cautious methods do.
- **Technical:** we are the least safe arm wherever the comparison is run —
  5.0% violating on rdf, 0.5% on dar, against 0.0% for all four baselines. Worst
  excursion 1.54× the limit against their 0.23–0.33×.
- **δ is not a dial** (§3.4o): tightening it costs return and buys no safety.

### 24. Things that did not survive contact with a bigger sample
- Joint Simulate+Plan training: claimed as a contribution, did not survive
  (t = +0.66, +1.00, identical on three PDE families). The figure set was
  **replaced**, not patched.
- Explanation faithfulness: 0.47 vs 0.18 post-hoc, t = +21 — and a permutation
  control showed the briefs carry **zero** state-specific information. The
  faithfulness term is net harmful (§3.4j).
- The twin-loop effect shrank to a third at 75× the sample (§5.2a).
- **Say why this slide exists:** three of these were found by applying the
  project's own rule to itself, and all three moved a published number *down*.

### 25. Working rules we had to write down
- *A sample-efficiency claim is a comparison, not a ratio.* Run the baselines at
  your budget before quoting a ratio about theirs. (Rule 65)
- *A reliability figure is a ratio, and the denominator is an argument.* (Rule 103)
- *A metric key that exists is not the metric you wanted.* We paired one
  policy's benefit with another policy's cost for three runs because both keys
  were present and plausible. (Rule 104)
- *Check that the knob you are turning reaches the thing you are turning it
  for.* A seven-hour budget sweep turned out to be one experiment with three
  labels. (Rule 105)
- *No single-run comparisons.* Caught three times in one session. (Rule 100)

### 26. Limitations
- The flood environments are synthetic in their forcing ensemble; the frontier's
  *shape* is trustworthy, its absolute numbers are not.
- The pathwise gradient on the flood twin is **one-step truncated** — state is
  detached between control intervals, so credit assignment does not cross one.
- Digital-twin claim 4 — correcting the model from *intervention outcomes* — is
  **not demonstrated**. State correction is (§5.2). Outcome correction is not.
- Evacuation ensemble has six members, so "robust" means "nothing in these six
  broke it".

### 27. What next
- Close the twin loop on intervention outcomes, not just state — the one twin
  claim still open.
- A retrospective shadow: replay historical events using only data available at
  the time, and score the model against what actually happened.
- The eight-seed saturation-guard result on the polder (running).
- Whether α does anything once the policy is not pinned at a corner — the first
  honest test of the mixing rule on this environment.

### 28. One sentence
> **Components that score well on their own metrics do not add up to a decision
> that works; measure against reality on a schedule, and you can both recover
> the lost performance and say how often you will fail.**
